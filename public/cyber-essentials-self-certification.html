The assessment

The five controls you’ll be assessed against

Cyber Essentials is not a broad audit. It tests five specific technical controls, and the scheme is explicit about what each one requires. Here is what you’ll need to evidence.

01
Firewalls

Boundary and host firewalls in place, default passwords changed, and every inbound rule documented with a business justification.

02
Secure configuration

Unused accounts and software removed, default credentials replaced, and auto-run disabled on removable media.

03
Security update management

Only vendor-supported software in use, with high and critical updates applied within 14 days of release.

04
User access control

A unique account per user, administrator rights granted only where genuinely needed, and multi-factor authentication on all cloud services.

05
Malware protection

Anti-malware running on every in-scope device, or an approved allow-listing or sandboxing approach in its place.

Before you buy

Conditions and scope

Two things catch people out: how the price band is decided, and what counts as an in-scope device. Both are worth two minutes now.

How your price band is decided

Your band is set by the total number of employees across your entire organisation — including part-time staff, contractors on your payroll and any sites outside the certification scope. It is not set by the number of people or devices being certified. Selecting a band below your actual headcount will fail assessment, and the difference becomes payable before your certificate is issued.

What’s in scope

  • All end-user devices, including home workers’ laptops and BYOD phones used for work
  • All cloud services holding organisational data, including SaaS applications
  • Internet-facing infrastructure: servers, routers and firewalls
  • Whole-organisation certification, or a sub-scope separated by network and account boundaries

Not included in this package

  • Hands-on remediation — we tell you what to fix, you or your IT provider fix it
  • Independent technical testing and device sampling (that’s Cyber Essentials Plus)
  • Policy writing, device configuration or software licences
  • Further submissions once the free resubmission window has passed
Questions

Frequently asked

How long does certification take?

Most organisations certify within 10 to 14 days. The fixed parts are ours: portal access the next working day, technical review inside 48 hours. The variable is how quickly you answer the questionnaire. Fast-track is sometimes available — ask before you buy rather than after.

What happens if our first submission doesn’t pass?

You get one free resubmission within two working days of the result, provided you correct the issues the assessor raised. Past that window a new submission is chargeable at the full band price. This is why we review everything before it goes in — the free window is short.

Do home workers’ devices count?

Yes. Any device used to access organisational data or services is in scope, including personal laptops and phones under a BYOD arrangement. An ISP-supplied home router is generally out of scope, but the device’s own software firewall is not — it has to be enabled and configured.

Can we certify just one part of the business?

You can scope to a clearly separated part of the organisation, but the sub-scope has to be genuinely segregated by network and account boundaries — a department that shares a domain with everyone else doesn’t qualify. Your price band is still set by total headcount.

Is the £25,000 insurance automatic?

It’s included for UK-domiciled organisations with an annual turnover under £20m, and you can opt out at submission if you already hold cyber cover. Cover starts when the certificate is issued, not when you buy.

What’s the difference between this and Cyber Essentials Plus?

Cyber Essentials is a verified self-assessment. Plus adds independent technical testing — external vulnerability scanning and hands-on device sampling carried out by an assessor. You need Cyber Essentials in place before Plus can be issued, so this is the right starting point either way.
